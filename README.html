Gem Rush Duel

A real-time, two-player Canvas game for the Internet Computing group project. Built from the supplied game, authentication and Socket.IO labs with a single authoritative Node.js server.

Installation and startup

Use Node.js 22 or later with npm. In the gem-rush-duel directory, run exactly:

npm install
node game_server.js

Open http://localhost:8000. There is no build, migration, seed or asset-generation step. Port 8000 must be free. Stop an older lab server first if it is already using this port.

Register two accounts and sign in using two different browsers, separate browser profiles, or a normal window and a private session. Ordinary tabs in the same profile share login cookies. Multiple private windows can also share cookies, so they are not automatically independent. Click Find a Match in both sessions.

For two computers on the same LAN, run only one server and use http://SERVER_LAN_IP:8000 on both. The host firewall must allow that port. LAN operation is supported by the binding; the automated checks use one machine. All browser scripts, graphics, fonts and audio are bundled locally; gameplay needs no internet after dependencies are installed.

The server initializes missing JSON files in data/. No existing lab accounts are copied. Paths are resolved from the project, so launching its absolute server path from another directory also works. Stop the server with Ctrl+C. Restarting ends live matches and clears in-memory sessions; accounts and rankings survive. You may need to sign in again.

Controls and complete rules

  • WASD or arrow keys: move, including normalized diagonal movement. Release on blur, hidden document, disconnect or match end. Players have solid circular bodies and can block one another.
  • Win: first to at least 50 points wins immediately. The round lasts 120 seconds after the shared 3–2–1 countdown. At the deadline, higher score wins; equal scores draw. No scoring is allowed at or after the deadline.
  • Gems: green 1, red 2, yellow 3, purple 5 points. Gems animate and last 6 seconds. Eight begin the match; another spawns approximately every 650 ms, with at most 12. Color probabilities are 50%, 25%, 15%, 10% respectively. Gems pop in when they spawn, rare yellow/purple gems glow, and gems blink in their last 1.3 seconds.
  • Gem Frenzy: when 20 seconds remain, a banner announces the Gem Frenzy, the arena border glows gold, and gems spawn twice as often (every 325 ms) until the end.
  • Contested pickups: the nearest eligible player's body gets a touched gem or power-up. An exact distance tie goes to the first FIFO match slot. A pickup is removed once. Stunned, frozen and disconnected players cannot collect.
  • Fire: three animated hazards move and bounce inside the play area. Contact costs 2 points and stuns for 1 second. Scores clamp to zero. Each player has a 1.5-second fire-hit cooldown. The heat rises: a fourth fire joins when 60 seconds remain and a fifth when 30 seconds remain, each announced by a banner.
  • Streaks: collecting gems without being hit by fire or frozen builds a streak. Milestones (3, 5, 10 …) are celebrated on screen and the best streak appears in the statistics.
  • Speed Boots: automatically give 1.5× movement speed for 5 seconds. Another pair refreshes the expiry, without stacking speed.
  • Ice Crystal: automatically freezes the opponent for 2 seconds. Another hit refreshes the expiry. A successful application increases the opponent's times-frozen count; cheat immunity blocks both the effect and count.
  • Power-ups: a random boots/crystal pickup spawns every 8 seconds, lasts 10 seconds and has a maximum of two present. A stunned or frozen player cannot move until both effects expire.
  • C: toggles the intentional cheat feature during play. Holding C does not repeatedly toggle it. Typing in forms never triggers game shortcuts.
  • Sound: click Sound on/off. The preference persists in this browser. Ten local sound files: background music, gem collection, fire hit, Speed Boots, Ice Crystal freeze, cheat toggle, 3-2-1 countdown beeps (also a tick each second in the final 10 seconds), GO, win jingle and game-over (loss/draw). Audio unlocks on a gesture; playback failures do not block a round. After a reload, click the sound control to authorize audio again if needed.

CHEAT MODE ON

Cheat mode gives 2× normal speed and 3× gem points, prevents fire damage/stun and ice freeze, and clears existing stun/freeze. Cheat speed overrides boots (maximum 2×), but the boots expiry clock keeps running. Switching cheat off restores remaining boots speed or normal speed. Cleared effects do not return. A large flashing CHEAT MODE ON banner appears over the arena for the cheating player, who also gets a magenta aura and sparkle trail. Both browsers show the cheat status in the scoreboard; results record any cheat use, even if turned off before the finish. Cheat games count on the leaderboard.

Arena and balancing

Logical Canvas: 854 × 480, scaled by CSS without changing coordinates. Walkable bounds follow the supplied lab: x=60–800, y=165–420. Body centers are clamped with their radii inside this rectangle. Normal speed is 180 logical pixels/second. Spawn attempts avoid bodies and hazards, fall back to a bounded grid search and skip a spawn if there is no safe point. All tuning is in server/config.js. These are prototype choices, not additional assignment requirements.

Accounts, matchmaking and recovery

Usernames are 3–20 ASCII letters/digits/underscores, normalized to lowercase for uniqueness. Display names are trimmed to 1–24 characters. Passwords are 8–128 characters, confirmed exactly, never trimmed, and stored only as Argon2id hashes. Eight approved avatar IDs are accepted (fox, cat, frog, owl, bunny, bear, panda, penguin); each one is also the player's in-game character. Both the UI and server validate fields. A case-insensitive uniqueness check runs inside the serialized account write.

Successful sign-in regenerates and saves an express-session identity before Socket.IO connects. Cookies are HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax and compatible with localhost HTTP. A fresh random process secret is used unless SESSION_SECRET is supplied. Sessions live in memory. Socket identity always comes from the HTTP session; each incoming event revalidates the session and current controlling connection. Sign-out uses POST, invalidates the session, and disconnects its sockets.

Matchmaking is FIFO and contains at most one entry per account. Two distinct online users form a unique private room. Assets must load and both clients must acknowledge readiness within 15 seconds. The server schedules one shared three-second countdown; gameplay and the two-minute deadline start after it.

The newest connection for an account takes control. Its older tab is notified and disconnected; that old disconnect cannot delete the replacement. A reload reconnects the same account to the same active match, restoring a full snapshot and clearing input.

  • During active play, a dropped connection has 10 seconds to reconnect. Its input clears immediately and the match clock continues. The opponent sees a reconnection countdown.
  • If one player's grace period expires with the opponent connected, the opponent wins by forfeit. If both remain disconnected until their grace periods expire (or the round ends while both are absent), the match is cancelled without rankings.
  • A deliberate Leave or Sign out during play is an immediate forfeit if the opponent is present. If both have left, cancel without awarding a win.
  • A disconnect/leave during loading or countdown cancels the start and returns the available opponent to the lobby, without a result.
  • If time expires during one player's grace period, the authoritative timer result ends the round; the match is not extended.

Visual feedback

There are 8 playable characters: fox, cat, frog, owl, bunny, bear, panda and penguin. The avatar a player picks at registration is the character they play as. Each character has its own shape and features (ears, tail, beak, eye patches and so on), a walk cycle in four directions, and an idle animation with breathing and blinking. If both players pick the same character, the second player automatically gets that character's alternate colours (for example, an orange fox against a white arctic fox), so the two players always look different. The match intro shows both animated characters in a VS screen. Gem pickups burst into particles in the gem's colour with a floating +points. Fire hits throw sparks, show the points lost, and shake the screen of the player who was hit. Frozen players are encased in an ice block with a countdown, and stunned players have orbiting stars. Speed Boots leave a dust trail. The timer pulses red in the final 10 seconds, with a large countdown in the arena. Name tags move apart when the players overlap.

Results, persistence and rematches

Results contain 17 statistics for both players: final score, each color's actual gem count, total gems, best gem streak, boots, ice, total power-ups, rivals frozen, fire hits, points lost to fire, times frozen, distance run, cheat use and win/loss/draw. The better value in each row is highlighted. Below the all-time top 10, each player sees their own all-time rank (for example, #3 of 12 players). The leaderboard also shows games played, and a top-5 Hall of Fame is shown in the lobby before matches. Gem counts are physical pickups; the cheat multiplier only changes points. The winner is ranked first even on a lower-score forfeit. Draws show both tied for first.

The all-time top 10 uses best final score descending, then total wins descending, then canonical username ascending. Completed forfeits retain each player's actual final score and award one win to the remaining opponent. Draws add no wins. Completed cheat games count. Cancelled/abandoned games do not update records.

data/accounts.json and data/leaderboard.json are outside the public directory. Each file has a serialized read/modify/write queue with temporary-file-plus-rename replacement. The leaderboard stores completed match IDs so the same result cannot increment wins twice. A corrupt/invalid file stops startup with a clear error and is preserved for recovery. A failed result save is reported to both browsers; the prototype does not pretend it succeeded.

One Play again click shows Waiting for opponent and both readiness states. Two clicks create one fresh ID and reset scores, statistics, effects, cheat, input, entities and timing before loading/countdown. Back to Home cancels that participant's rematch eligibility and releases them for matchmaking. Results are retained for reconnects for two minutes, then room membership and the match are cleaned up. The displayed result remains readable after this expiry, but rematching is disabled.

Code map and event contract

server/auth.js          Validation, Argon2 and sessions
server/storage.js       Serialized atomic JSON persistence
server/config.js        Rules, bounds and timing defaults
server/game.js          Testable numeric game simulation
server/matchmaking.js   Rooms, queue, sockets and lifecycle
public/index.html       Home, game, results and instructions
public/scripts/         Adapted lab sprites, renderer, audio and UI
public/assets/          Bundled art, font, sounds and licenses
public/vendor/          Local jQuery; Socket.IO is served by Node
tests/                  Rules, HTTP, sockets, persistence and browser QA

The server owns positions, scores, entities, effects, timing, statistics and results. Clients send only intentions. The simulation uses a 30 Hz fixed step and broadcasts approximately 30 snapshots/second. Catch-up work is capped at five steps to avoid event-loop spirals; deadlines still use a monotonic epoch-like server clock. Rendering uses requestAnimationFrame and interpolates players and fires 80 ms behind snapshots. There is no local prediction. Clock pings estimate server offset from minimum round-trip time.

Obsolete match IDs and malformed inputs are ignored. Rate limits cap each socket at 70 incoming packets/second and 20 non-input commands/second. HTTP password operations are limited to 40 attempts per IP/minute. There are no arbitrary-room joins, score-setting, entity-spawning, or debug HTTP/socket endpoints.

How the supplied labs were adapted

The Sprite factory, shadow/draw/update logic, all Player directional sequences, all four Gem sequences and the Fire sequence come from lab3_gem_rush. Its Canvas loop, audio, keyboard-release and arena bounds were retained and adapted. The game_room archive supplies the register/sign-in/validate/Argon2/session/JSON pattern and jQuery form-page flow. The hand_game lab supplies the same-server Socket.IO pattern, presence/readiness/start/end/disconnect flow. The extra game_server archive and dinosaur/whackamole examples were inspected for request handling and interaction examples. No framework, game engine, frontend build pipeline or external data service was added. See CREDITS.txt for a file-by-file map and licenses.

Verification

npm test

This runs Node's built-in test runner and socket.io-client. It uses temporary data directories and ephemeral ports, with no changes to real accounts. Deterministic simulation tests cover countdown, normalized movement, bounds/collisions, contested pickups, colors, expiry, hazards, effects, cheat interactions, timing, terminal idempotency and fresh state. HTTP/socket tests cover validation, concurrent registration, hashing, sessions, unusual usernames, authorization, four-user room isolation, queue cancellation, duplicate tabs, reconnect/forfeit, abandonment, rematches and storage integrity.

The optional real-browser test uses Playwright, installed only for development:

npm install --no-save playwright
npx playwright install chromium
npm run test:browser

Alternatively set PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE to an installed Playwright module and CHROME_PATH to a Chrome executable. These are optional test settings, never startup requirements. Browser QA creates two independent contexts, registers/signs in through the UI, plays with actual keyboard events until a target-score win, reloads mid-match, and checks two rematches, forfeits, results and navigation. It observes simulation state to guide input and never changes scores or positions. Screenshots and its JSON report are written to ignored test-output/.

Prototype limits

  • Desktop keyboard controls are required; touch movement is not implemented. Smaller displays can scroll instructions/results; Canvas scales with logical coordinates preserved.
  • One server process, local writable disk and in-memory sessions. No multi-process clustering or production session store. Server restarts terminate matches. A process crash before a final result is saved can lose that result.
  • Designed for localhost/LAN coursework. HTTPS hosting, distributed load testing, account recovery and deployment are outside this deliverable.
  • Interpolation adds about 80 ms of visual delay. Under long server stalls the simulation caps catch-up movement but does not extend the match deadline.
  • Modern Chrome was used for automated browser QA. Other browsers and actual speaker output were not manually verified. The audio files were loaded/decoded in Chrome.
  • Persistent completed-match IDs grow with usage; this small JSON prototype has no archival/pruning job.

A demonstration under five minutes

  1. 0:00–0:35: Show the startup commands and home instructions. Register/sign in in two independent sessions and point out names/avatars.
  2. 0:35–1:00: Find a match in each browser; show pairing, loading and the same 3–2–1 countdown.
  3. 1:00–2:10: Show movement mirrored in the other browser, all gem colors, moving fire and stun, boots speed and ice freeze. Show the same scores and timer.
  4. 2:10–2:50: Toggle C on and off, demonstrate immunity/speed/triple points, then collect to 50. Show matching results, gem counts, cheat disclosure and leaderboard.
  5. 2:50–3:35: One rematch click waits; two start a fresh countdown with reset state. Reload one browser and show recovery, then leave to demonstrate a forfeit.
  6. 3:35–4:20: Return home, show online states and sign-out. Briefly show the lab adaptation credits and passing tests.
  7. 4:20–4:50: If time permits, use two additional independent profiles to show two isolated matches. End within five minutes.
